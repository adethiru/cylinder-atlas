    What makes ATLAS different

    Built on your thesis, not a generic database

    Grata, SourceScrub and Inven return lists of companies. ATLAS scores every target against this acquisition thesis: recurring rental, succession, route density, supplier independence and safety.

    Evidence you can audit

    Every fact the Scout records quotes the company's own website word-for-word. Invented facts are rejected in code, so the investment case only uses verified evidence.

    Roll-up economics built in

    Each target comes with an indicative valuation, the multiple arbitrage, route-density synergies and a live bridge to $1B. The output is a plan, not just a list of names.

    Ownership intelligence

    Companies already owned by the majors or by Meritus, AWG and Holston are excluded, each with the reason. PE-owned rivals are flagged with their exit windows as secondary-buyout options.

    A sector-specific universe

    700+ companies from the 2026 IWDC directory, EIGA, trade press and a deep dive on Canada, each with a public source. Country risk is filtered for you.

    Safe, cheap, human-in-the-loop

    The agents are guarded against prompt injection, limited to an allow-list of sites and capped on budget. Outreach letters are only drafts until you approve them. Every token is metered.

    01

    Why industrial gas distribution

    Recurring revenue

    Monthly cylinder rental plus scheduled refills (O₂, N₂, H₂, Ar, CO₂). ~50–60% of EBITDA is contractually recurring; ~70–80% repeating.

    Source: investor's industry analysis

    Sticky customers

    Gas is cheap relative to the cost of failure. Multi-year contracts, on-site telemetry and physical cylinders make switching costly.

    Non-cyclical demand

    Healthcare (medical oxygen, biologics), food & beverage (CO₂, N₂), water treatment, fabrication, labs and electronics.

    Low capex, tax efficient

    Steel cylinders last 30–50+ years; hydrostatic testing every 5–10 years. Section 179 write-offs front-load the tax shield.

    Source: investor's industry analysis

    Fragmented, proven roll-up

    ~1,000 US independents (500+ GAWDA members). Meritus (AEA) reached ~$350m sales by 2023 and targets $1bn.

    Sources: investor's industry analysis; GAWDA ↗; Meritus Gas Partners ↗

    Low AI-disruption risk

    A physical, safety-critical, last-mile service with diversified SME customers and low concentration.

    02

    Value-creation levers

    Route density

    Tuck-ins near existing branches share trucks, drivers and fill plants: +2–4 margin points.

    Procurement

    Cylinders, gas and vehicles bought at scale; IWDC co-op purchasing (~$4.5bn).

    Source: investor's industry analysis; IWDC ↗
    Multiple arbitrage

    Tuck-ins at ~5–7x EBITDA; a scaled platform is worth ~9–12x.

    Planning benchmarks (thesis); verify with comps
    Cross-sell

    Bulk, specialty and medical gas plus welding hardgoods across the network.

    03

    Key risks and how we mitigate them

    Tier-1 supplier concentration

    Linde, Air Liquide, Air Products, Messer dominate bulk supply.

    IWDC co-op purchasing, dual-sourcing, surcharge & pass-through clauses; producers need downstream volume.
    Hazmat driver shortage

    ~300k qualified US drivers; wage inflation.

    Telemetry-driven dispatch, home-daily routes, CDL academy, retention bonuses, denser routes.
    Safety & regulation

    PHMSA, OSHA, EPA; high-pressure, hazardous gases.

    CGA standards, PHMSA/OSHA record checks in diligence, group EHS lead, training and insurance.
    Key-person & succession

    Family owners; customer and staff loyalty at risk.

    Rollover equity, earn-outs, keep the local brand and branch managers, owner transition period.
    Roll-up competition

    Meritus, AWG, Arc3, Holston, B&J, Airgas bid for the same owners.

    Proprietary owner relationships, speed and certainty of close, white-space regions first.
    Integration

    Systems, cylinder tracking, pricing, procurement.

    Common ERP and cylinder-tracking platform, centralised procurement, 100-day plan.
    International expansion

    Europe (ADR rules), China & SE Asia (ownership limits, FX, incumbents).

    Enter via a local platform partner, minority-to-majority structures, avoid capex-heavy on-site/tonnage.
    04

    Where the targets are

    1 · Independents — primary

    Family and founder-owned distributors facing succession. North America (IWDC co-op: 150+ members, 850+ locations), Europe incl. Nordics, Middle East & Africa, Latin America and Asia Pacific.

    2 · PE-owned — secondary buyouts

    Sponsor-owned rivals whose hold is maturing: Meritus Gas Partners (AEA, since Dec-2020, ~30 companies, 100+ locations; CEO expects recapitalisations), AirFirst Korea (IMM, ~7.5 yrs), B&J / American Gas Group (Cordatus).

    3 · Rival bidders — context

    Meritus, American Welding & Gas, Arc3, Holston, Airgas and the Tier-1 majors. Companies they already own are shown greyed out and excluded from the plan.
